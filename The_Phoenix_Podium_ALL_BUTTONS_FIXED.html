Debate Dictionary

Useful debating words explained simply.

Motion

The debate statement or topic being argued.

Proposition

The side that supports the motion.

Opposition

The side that argues against the motion.

Rebuttal

A response that challenges the other side’s argument.

Evidence

Facts, examples, statistics or expert views used to support a point.

POI

Point of Information: a short question or challenge during a speech.

Burden

What a side needs to prove to win the debate.

Weighing

Explaining why your argument matters more than the other side’s.

Impact

The result or consequence of an argument.

Signposting

Clear phrases that guide the audience through your speech.

Framing

Setting up how the debate should be understood.

Extension

A new argument that adds something important to the debate.

Logical Fallacy Trainer

Learn common mistakes in arguments.

Straw Man

Misrepresenting someone’s argument to make it easier to attack.

Example: “You want less homework, so you must hate learning.”

False Dilemma

Pretending there are only two choices when there are more.

Example: “Either ban phones completely or accept chaos.”

Ad Hominem

Attacking the person instead of the argument.

Example: “Your point is wrong because you are young.”

Slippery Slope

Claiming one small step will definitely lead to disaster.

Example: “If pupils use AI once, nobody will ever learn again.”

Hasty Generalisation

Making a big claim from too little evidence.

Example: “One zoo was bad, so all zoos are bad.”

Appeal to Emotion

Using feelings instead of strong reasoning.

Example: “Only a heartless person would disagree.”
